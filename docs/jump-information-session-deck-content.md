# JUMP 2026 Information Session

## Cover

**JUMP 2026**

**From activity to enterprise**

Strategy & Innovation Genius Track | Emmanuel Tarfa

## Slide 1

### The issue is not ambition. It is enterprise architecture.

- Many capable founders are working hard without a clear operating design beneath the work.
- JUMP helps convert ideas, expertise, and market opportunity into decisions, structure, and executable priorities.
- The objective is not to give people more theory; it is to help them see what to do next and why.

**Presenter cue:** Open by naming the room’s reality: capable people can still be unclear about what they are building, how it makes money, or which decision matters most.

## Slide 2

### My work is to improve the quality of your decisions.

- Identify the actual business question before prescribing activity.
- Test the relationship between founder, market, business model, and operating reality.
- Turn insight into a sequence of decisions the founder can execute.

**Presenter cue:** Position yourself as an advisor who diagnoses before recommending. Use one brief example from your own experience rather than a long case-study catalogue.

## Slide 3

### A business becomes real when its choices agree.

- **Strategic intent:** What are you trying to build, for whom, and to what end?
- **Commercial logic:** Why will customers choose it, pay for it, and stay with it?
- **Operating capacity:** What must the founder, team, systems, and capital base be able to carry?

**Presenter cue:** Pause after each question. Let participants recognise where their own business is currently ambiguous.

## Slide 4

### You do not need every answer today. You need the right next question.

- The Current State Assessment gives the first picture of the founder, business, market, and direction of travel.
- The assessment is not a final examination; it tells us where deeper work is needed.
- Every live session begins from the evolving reality of the people in the room.

**Presenter cue:** Explain that the platform is an advisory tool, not administrative theatre. Participants are not being scored for its own sake.

## Slide 5

### The programme is designed to move as the room becomes clearer.

- The agenda gives structure, but the emphasis can shift when a recurring live issue requires attention.
- Cohort conversations, diagnostic evidence, and founder questions inform how you go deeper.
- Flexibility is disciplined: the goal is greater relevance without losing strategic coherence.

**Presenter cue:** This is the slide that establishes your ability to manoeuvre. Say plainly: “I pay attention to how the room is thinking, and I adapt the work to what will move people forward.”

## Slide 6

### Foundation: shared clarity for the decisions that come first.

- A **shared group advisory engagement** for people who need a stronger view of their business before they add complexity.
- Participants leave clearer on strategic intent, positioning, priorities, and the first decisions that deserve focus.
- The value is perspective: seeing one’s business through a rigorous framework and alongside other builders facing real decisions.

**Presenter cue:** Do not describe Foundation as private consulting. It is a deliberate group experience that creates clarity, challenge, and practical direction.

## Slide 7

### Engine Room: deeper work for businesses that need more diagnostic attention.

- Foundation thinking plus additional working sessions for founders whose questions require more unpacking.
- Participants are grouped because the diagnosis indicates a need for deeper work, not because of a generic tier label.
- The outcome is a more specific route through commercial logic, operating decisions, and execution priorities.

**Presenter cue:** Be explicit: the Engine Room can be intentionally small. If only a few people belong in that room, that allows the work to go deeper.

## Slide 8

### Boardroom: private time when the business needs your direct attention.

- Foundation and Engine Room experience, plus **three private 90-minute strategy sessions** with Emmanuel Tarfa.
- Built for founders who need concentrated discussion on their own company, decisions, constraints, and next moves.
- The private sessions translate the shared learning into business-specific strategic choices.

**Presenter cue:** State the distinction cleanly: Boardroom is the only pathway that includes private strategy sessions.

## Slide 9

### The same method produces different kinds of progress.

| Pathway | What the participant gains | What changes next |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | Clarity on the business they are building and the decisions that matter first | A more confident strategic direction and priority list |
| **Engine Room** | A deeper diagnosis of the issues holding the business back | A more specific operating and commercial route forward |
| **Boardroom** | Direct strategic attention on the founder’s own business | Decisions, deep dives, and private accountability over three sessions |

**Presenter cue:** Explain that people are not purchasing more slides. They are selecting the level of advisory attention their business needs.

## Slide 10

### The output is not a conversation. It is a plan you can run.

- We discuss what the business should do, why it matters, and what must happen first.
- Sophisticated AI can help translate structured discussion into usable plans, actions, and working artefacts.
- Technology speeds up documentation and execution support; judgment, accountability, and decisions remain human work.

**Presenter cue:** Make the AI point practical, not fashionable. “We do not stop at insight. We turn the work into something you can use.”

## Slide 11

### What you should expect to leave with.

- **Clarity:** a sharper view of your strategic intent, position, and immediate business problem.
- **Decision capability:** a way to make difficult trade-offs on market, model, team, AI, funding, risk, and execution.
- **Momentum:** an actionable sequence that helps you move from discussion to disciplined execution.

**Presenter cue:** Frame this as advisory outcomes. Do not promise a guaranteed commercial result; promise stronger decision-making and execution readiness.

## Slide 12

### JUMP is designed to be practical, supported, and responsive.

- Your portal keeps the brief, assessment, working materials, and later resources in one place.
- If you have a technical issue, question, or point of uncertainty, ask directly or email Emmanuel; screenshots are helpful for technical issues.
- The experience works on mobile, though a laptop or desktop gives the strongest working experience.

**Presenter cue:** Reassure the room without overexplaining the technology. Use a calm phrase: “The platform is there to support the work, not get in the way of it.”

## Slide 13

### The practical question is: what does your business need next?

- Choose the route that matches the depth of advisory attention your business now requires.
- Complete the Current State Assessment honestly; it helps shape the work that follows.
- Come into the sessions prepared to ask the question that has been holding your business still.

**Presenter cue:** This is the call to action. Invite questions, but keep the decision simple: choose the right pathway and begin the diagnostic work.

## Slide 14
